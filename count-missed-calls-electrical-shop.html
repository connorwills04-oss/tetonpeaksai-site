How to count the calls your electrical shop misses

It is easy to guess how many calls you miss. A one-week count takes a few minutes a day and tells you when calls slip and whether it is worth fixing.

Where to look

Find your missed calls

  • Your cell phone's recent calls. Missed calls are usually marked. Check every phone that rings for the business.
  • Your business phone system or carrier account. Many have a call log or report online.
  • Voicemail. Count voicemails separately from hang-ups.
  • Call forwarding. If calls forward to someone else after hours, check their phone too.

Easy to miss

Calls that don't show up as missed

  • Busy line. If you were already on a call, a second caller may hear a busy signal or go straight to voicemail. Check if your phone system logs these.
  • Old numbers. A number still on a truck, a yard sign, or an old listing may ring a phone no one checks.
  • Forwarded calls. Calls forwarded to a helper or a family member after hours may be missed on their phone, not yours.
  • Hang-ups in a few seconds. Short calls still count. The caller may have tried someone else next.

Example

What a week of notes might look like

An owner writes down every missed call for a week. Most of them land between 10 AM and 3 PM, while the crew is on jobs. Few callers left a voicemail. Two of the numbers called twice the same day. By Friday, the owner can see the problem is midday calls, not after-hours calls.

FAQ

Questions owners ask

How do I find out how many calls my shop misses?

For one week, check every phone that rings for the business, your carrier or phone system call log, and voicemail. Write down the day, time, number, whether they left a message, and whether you called back.

Should I count every missed call?

Count each number once a day so repeat tries don't inflate the count, and leave out spam you can spot.

How long should I track missed calls?

One normal week is a good start. If your work is seasonal, a busy week tells you more.

Call forwarding for electricians: busy, no-answer, and all calls

Forwarding decides where a call goes when you can't pick up. It is how a shop can hand calls to an on-call phone, an answering service, or a missed-call text back, while customers keep calling the same number.

The types

Three kinds of forwarding

All calls

Every call goes straight to another number. Your phone doesn't ring. Good for a vacation or a fixed after-hours handoff.

No answer

Your phone rings first. If no one picks up after a set number of rings, the call moves on. This is the usual pick for a shop that is on jobs all day.

Busy

If you are already on a call, the next caller moves on instead of hearing a busy signal or going to voicemail.

Turning it on

How to set it up

Carriers usually let you turn forwarding on with a short code or in your account. Verizon, for example, documents conditional forwarding for busy or unanswered calls after a few rings. Codes and steps differ by carrier and by phone system, so check your carrier's help page or call them.

If your business number is on a VoIP or office phone system, forwarding is usually a setting in that system instead.

Before you rely on it

Test it once a month

  • Call your business number from a phone that is not in your contacts and let it ring out.
  • Check that it lands where you expect and that the right person gets the alert.
  • Check again after any change to your phone plan, phone, or office system.

Example

Forwarding for a two-person shop

A two-person shop sets no-answer forwarding on the office line. During the day, missed calls move to a texting number and each caller gets a text. After 6 PM, the shop switches to all-calls forwarding to the owner's phone for emergencies. Once a month, the owner calls the line to make sure both still work.

Questions owners ask

What is conditional call forwarding?

It sends a call to another number only when your phone is busy or no one answers after a set number of rings. Forwarding all calls sends every call without ringing you first.

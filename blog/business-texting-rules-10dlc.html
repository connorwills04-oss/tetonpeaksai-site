Business texting rules in plain English

What small businesses should know before texting customers: carrier registration (10DLC), consent, and STOP opt-outs.

Home / Blog / Business texting rules in plain English

Texting customers works. It also comes with rules. Here are the basics, in plain English. This is general information, not legal advice.

1. Register with the carriers (A2P 10DLC)

Business texts sent from a regular 10-digit number have to be registered with the phone carriers. You register your business and what you'll text about. Unregistered texts can be blocked. Approval can take from a couple of days to a few weeks.

2. Get consent

Only text people who gave you their number and agreed to hear from you. Marketing texts need clearer permission than replies to someone who just contacted you. The federal rules come from the Telephone Consumer Protection Act (TCPA), and the FCC's rules at 47 CFR 64.1200.

3. Honor STOP and HELP

Every program should let people reply STOP to opt out and HELP for help. When someone texts STOP, stop texting them. The wireless industry's guidelines (the CTIA Messaging Principles and Best Practices) spell this out.

4. Keep it relevant

Text about what people signed up for. Identify your business in the message. Keep it short.

When we set up texting for a client, we handle the carrier registration and build in STOP and HELP. If your texting involves special cases, like health info or marketing lists, talk to your attorney.

Should electricians give free estimates? A simple way to decide

Some shops give free estimates, some charge, and many do both depending on the job. Electricians on Reddit describe spending whole days on quotes that go nowhere. Here is a way to decide, and to screen requests before you drive out.

Sort the request

Three kinds of estimate requests

RequestOften handled asWhy
Small, simple job like a fixture or outletPrice from questions and photosLittle risk, fast answer
Bigger planned job like a panel or chargerFree visit, or a fee credited if they hire youWorth a trip, but screen first
Troubleshooting a problemA service call with a feeFinding the cause is the work

Common patterns, not rules. Your shop sets its own policy.

Screen first

Questions to ask before a free visit

  • What is the job, and where is it?
  • Can you text a few photos? See how to ask for photos before a visit.
  • When do you want it done?
  • Do you own the home?
  • Are you getting other quotes? It is fine to ask.

If you charge

How to say it without losing the job

Say it early and plainly, before you book the visit. If you credit the fee when they hire you, say that too. A clear fee up front loses fewer jobs than a surprise later.

For troubleshooting, call it what it is: a service call to find the problem. See price-shopping calls at an electrical shop.

Sample text

A reply to an estimate request

Estimate request

"Hi [name], this is [your shop name]. Happy to help. What is the job, and can you text a couple of photos? Some jobs we can price from photos. Others need a visit, and we'll tell you up front if there is a fee."

Sets expectations before the visit.

After the estimate

Free or not, follow up

An estimate with no follow-up can go to waste. A short check-in a few days later is often enough. See estimate follow-up texts for electricians and quote follow-up for trades.

Example

Cutting wasted estimate trips

A one-truck electrician starts asking for photos before every estimate. A light fixture swap gets priced by text the same day. A whole-home rewire gets a visit. A "my lights flicker" call is booked as a service call, with the fee stated up front.

FAQ

Questions owners ask

Do electricians charge for estimates?

Some do and some don't. Many give free estimates for planned jobs and charge a service call fee to troubleshoot a problem. Your shop sets its own policy.

How can I avoid wasted free estimate trips?

Ask a few questions and for photos by text first. Some small jobs can be priced from photos, and you will know which visits are worth the drive.

Should I credit the estimate fee if they hire me?

Some shops do. If you do, say it when you book the visit, so the fee feels fair.

What is the difference between an estimate and a service call?

An estimate prices a job the customer already knows they want. A service call finds and fixes a problem. Finding the cause is the work, so many shops charge for it.

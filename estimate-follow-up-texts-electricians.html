A schedule

A simple three-text schedule

  1. 01

    Day 2: check for questions

    The quote is fresh. Offer to explain anything.

  2. 02

    Day 7: make it easy to say yes

    Offer a start date or the next open slot.

  3. 03

    Day 14: close the loop

    Ask if they want to keep the quote open. A clear "not now" is useful too.

Sample texts

Wording for each step

Day 2

"Hi [name], it's [your shop name]. Any questions on the estimate for [job]? Happy to walk through it."

One question. No pressure.

Day 7

"Hi [name], [your shop name] here. We have an opening [day] for the [job]. Want us to hold it for you?"

A real next step, if you have one.

Day 14

"Hi [name], just checking one last time on the [job] estimate. Should we keep it open, or close it out for now? Either is fine."

Gives them a polite way out.

Sample texts, not results from a client. Replace the words in [brackets], and approve the final wording.

Ground rules

Keep follow-up friendly

  • Stop when they reply. Then a person takes over.
  • Three is plenty. More starts to feel like pressure.
  • Name the job. "The panel upgrade estimate" is clearer than "your quote."
  • Only text people who gave you their number and agreed to hear from you. Honor STOP. See business texting rules.

Call instead

When a phone call beats a text

  • Big or complex jobs. A service upgrade or a generator may need a real conversation.
  • They asked a question. If the reply is a question that needs explaining, call.
  • They prefer calls. Some customers say so. Write it down and respect it.
  • Something changed. If your price or schedule changed, tell them by phone first.

10DLC registration for electricians, explained

If your shop texts customers from a regular 10-digit number, the phone carriers want it registered first. This is called A2P 10DLC. Here is what it involves, what to have ready, and why it takes time. This is general information, not legal advice.

The basics

What 10DLC is

10DLC stands for 10-digit long code, a regular local-style phone number. A2P means application to person: texts sent by a business system, not typed one at a time on a phone. US carriers ask businesses that send these texts to register, so they can tell real businesses from spam. Unregistered texts can be blocked.

For the rules on consent, STOP, and HELP, see business texting rules in plain English.

Two parts

What gets registered

Your business (the brand)

Your legal business name, tax ID (EIN), business address, website, and a contact. The details need to match your tax records.

What you'll text about (the campaign)

A short description of your texts, like replying to missed calls. Sample messages. How people agree to get texts. Your STOP and HELP replies.

Have it ready

What an electrical shop should gather first

  • Your exact legal business name, as it appears on your EIN paperwork. A dba is fine, but the legal name has to match.
  • Your EIN. Sole proprietors without one may have a separate path with lower limits. Ask your texting provider.
  • A working website that matches your business name, with a privacy policy that covers texting.
  • A business email and phone number.
  • A plain description of who you'll text and why.

Timing

Why texting can take days or weeks to start

The carriers review each registration. That can take from a couple of days to a few weeks. Mismatched names, a missing website, or unclear sample messages can slow it down or get it sent back for changes.

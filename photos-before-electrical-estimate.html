How to ask customers for photos before an electrical visit

A few photos can tell you if a job is simple, if you need a different part, or if you need to see it in person. Here is what to ask for, and how to ask so customers actually send them.

What to ask for

The photos that help most

PhotoWhy it helpsGood for
Panel with the door openShows breakers, open spaces, and the layoutEV chargers, panel upgrades, new circuits
Label inside the panel doorShows the make and ratingPanel upgrades, parts
The problem spot, close upShows the outlet, fixture, or switchService calls, repairs
A wide shot of the room or wallShows distance and accessNew outlets, lighting, chargers
The meter and outside wallShows where service comes inGenerators, service upgrades

Ask for two or three at most. More than that and people put it off.

How to ask

A text that gets photos back

Photo request

"Thanks, [name]. To get you a closer price, can you text us 2 photos? 1: your electrical panel with the door open. 2: the spot where you want the [job]. Please don't open the panel cover itself."

Numbered and specific. Add the safety note.

Keep it safe

What customers should not do

  • Tell them to open only the panel door, not remove the cover.
  • Tell them not to touch wires or anything that looks damaged.
  • If they see heat, sparks, or a burning smell, they should stop and follow your shop's safety instructions, like calling 911.

Use them well

Make the photos count

  1. 01

    Save them to the job

    Put the photos with the customer's job or quote, not just in one person's phone.

  2. 02

    Show the tech before the visit

    The right parts and the right ladder go on the truck the first time.

  3. 03

    Point to them in the quote

    Say what you saw: "From your photo, your panel has open spaces." It shows you looked.

Example

A fixture swap

A homeowner wants a ceiling fan where a light is now. The office asks for a photo of the fixture and a wide shot of the room. The photos show a tall ceiling, so the tech brings the taller ladder and the right mounting box, and finishes in one trip.

A hypothetical example, not a client story.

Good to know

When photos are not enough

Photos help you decide if a site visit is needed, and what to bring. They don't replace a visit for bigger or unclear jobs. Make sure the number you ask customers to text can receive photos. If it can't, give them one that can.

FAQ

Questions owners ask

What photos should an electrician ask for before an estimate?

The panel with the door open, the label inside the panel door, the problem spot up close, and a wide shot of the room or wall. Two or three is usually enough.

Is it safe to ask customers to photograph their panel?

Ask them to open only the panel door, not remove the cover, and not to touch wires. If they see heat, sparks, or a burning smell, they should stop and follow your shop's safety instructions.

Can I give a firm price from photos?

Sometimes for small, clear jobs. For bigger or unclear jobs, photos help you plan the visit, not replace it. That is your call.

How do I get customers to actually send photos?

Ask for two or three specific photos, number them, and say why: to get them a closer price faster.

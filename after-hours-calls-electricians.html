Your options

Five ways to handle calls you can't answer

Voicemail

Free and already set up. It works if callers leave a message and you call back fast.

Watch out: voicemail doesn't book appointments, and many callers won't leave one.

Forward to an on-call phone

A person on your team answers. Good for true emergency work.

Watch out: someone has to carry the phone, and calls still get missed when they're on a job or asleep.

Answering service

Live people answer your calls and take a message, for a fee. What else they do depends on the service and plan.

Watch out: ask what it costs and what counts as extra before you sign up.

Missed-call text back

No one answers, but every missed caller gets a friendly text from your shop right away, and you get an alert.

Good fit when most after-hours calls can wait until morning, but you don't want them calling the next shop.

AI receptionist

Our AI Front Desk answers your calls, asks the right questions, and books appointments 24/7.

Good fit when you get enough calls that you want them answered and booked, not just texted.

Storm days

When every truck is out

After a storm or an outage, calls can stack up while your crew is busy. Voicemail fills up, and an on-call phone only helps one caller at a time. A text back answers every missed caller right away and can ask for the address and what isn't working, so you can sort calls when you get a minute.

Example

A Saturday night call

It's 9:15 on a Saturday night. A homeowner calls because half the kitchen outlets stopped working. You're at dinner and miss it. Right away they get a text: "Hi, this is [your shop name]. We got your call. What's going on, and what's the address? We'll get back to you first thing Monday. If there is fire or immediate danger, call 911." They reply with the details. Monday morning you call them back with the details in hand.

A hypothetical example, not a client story.

Good to know

Text back is not dispatch.

  • A text after a missed call does not diagnose anything or decide what is an emergency. Your people make those calls.
  • If you take emergency calls, keep a way for those callers to reach a person, like an on-call phone.
  • You approve the wording. You can add a safety line, like telling callers to call 911 if there is fire or immediate danger.
  • Texts follow carrier rules. People can reply STOP to opt out and HELP for help. See business texting rules.

FAQ

Questions owners ask

What should an electrician do about after-hours calls?

Pick a plan for the calls you can't answer. Common options are voicemail, forwarding to an on-call phone, an answering service, missed-call text back, or an AI receptionist. You can mix them, like an on-call phone for emergencies and text back for everything else.

Does missed-call text back work at night and on weekends?

Yes. A text goes out on every missed call, any time. That includes nights, weekends, and storm days. You get an alert, and you choose when to reply.

Will it handle emergency calls?

No. A text after a missed call is not dispatch and it does not diagnose anything. Your people decide what is urgent and who goes out. If you take emergency calls, keep a way for those callers to reach a person.

What can the text tell after-hours callers?

You approve the wording. It can say you got the call, ask what is going on and the address, and say when you will call back. You can add a safety line, like telling callers to call 911 if there is fire or immediate danger.

Is voicemail enough after hours?

It can be, if you call back fast. But voicemail doesn't book appointments, and many callers won't leave one. A text gives them an easy way to reply.

Spam calls on your electrical shop's line: cut the noise, keep the customers

Once a shop shows up online, sales calls and robocalls can follow. Owners on Reddit describe missing real customers because they stopped answering unknown numbers. Here is how to cut the noise without losing new jobs.

The trap

Blocking unknown numbers blocks new customers too

A new customer is, by definition, a number you don't know. Phone settings that silence unknown callers send them to voicemail. On iPhone, Apple says the Silence option sends calls from numbers not in your contacts straight to voicemail.

Carrier blocking can also catch wanted calls. The FCC says that depending on your provider, a blocked call may go to voicemail, ring once, or give no notice at all.

Better options

Ways to cut spam without losing customers

  1. 01

    Use a business number for the public

    Put one number on your website and listings. Keep your personal cell for people you know.

  2. 02

    Turn on carrier labeling, not hard blocking

    Labels like "spam likely" let you choose. You can opt out of carrier blocking if it catches wanted calls, as the FCC notes.

  3. 03

    Text every missed caller

    If you let unknown numbers ring out, a text back still reaches the real customers. Robocalls don't reply. See missed-call text back for electricians.

  4. 04

    Consider a short menu

    A "press 1 for service" step stops many robocalls. If you run Google Local Services Ads, read how key presses affect lead charges. See Local Services Ads and missed calls.

  5. 05

    Check your spam folder

    Look at silenced and filtered calls once a day for real customers.

Calling back

Make sure your callbacks get through

The FCC suggests businesses use a valid outgoing number, leave a consistent callback number in messages, and keep customer callbacks on a different number from any telemarketing. If customers say your calls show up as spam, check with your carrier.

Know the size

Count the noise before you fix it

For one week, mark each call as customer, spam, or other. You will see how much time spam takes and how many customers you missed. See how to count your shop's missed calls.

Example

A new shop buried in sales calls

A new electrical shop lists its cell number online and gets sales calls all day. The owner starts ignoring unknown numbers and misses two real jobs. He moves the public listing to a business number, lets unknown calls ring out, and turns on text back. Real customers reply to the text. Robocalls don't.

FAQ

Questions owners ask

How do I stop spam calls on my business line?

Use a separate business number for the public, turn on your carrier's spam labeling, and check filtered calls daily. Hard blocking can catch real customers, so watch what it blocks.

Should I silence unknown callers on my phone?

Be careful. New customers are unknown numbers. On iPhone, the Silence option sends calls from numbers not in your contacts to voicemail. If you use it, make sure every missed caller still hears back.

Can missed-call text back tell spam from customers?

It texts back missed callers. Real customers tend to reply, and robocalls usually don't, so the replies show you who to call first.

Why do my callbacks show as spam?

Carriers watch calling patterns. The FCC suggests a valid outgoing number and a consistent callback number. If it keeps happening, check with your carrier.

Electrical service call intake: what to ask before anyone heads out

Whoever answers your phone, a person in the office, an answering service, or an AI receptionist, needs the same short list. Here is a plain intake script for residential service calls, in the order that works.

The script

Ask in this order

  1. 01

    Safety check

    Is there fire, smoke, sparks, or a burning smell right now? If yes, follow your shop's safety instructions, like telling the caller to call 911.

  2. 02

    Name and callback number

    Get a number that takes texts, so you can follow up by text.

  3. 03

    Address

    Full address, plus gate codes, pets, or parking notes.

  4. 04

    What is happening

    Whole house or one area? Did a breaker trip? Did it start after a storm, a new appliance, or other work?

  5. 05

    Power company check

    Are neighbors out too? If so, it may be a utility outage. Point them to their power company.

  6. 06

    Owner or renter

    Renters may need the owner to approve the work.

  7. 07

    Timing

    When can someone be there to let you in?

Leave it out

What the person answering should not do

  • Don't diagnose over the phone or tell the caller something is safe.
  • Don't promise an arrival time unless your schedule allows it.
  • Don't take card numbers by text.
  • Don't guess at prices. See how to handle price-shopping calls.

FAQ

Questions owners ask

What questions should an electrician ask on a service call?

Start with a safety check. Then get the name, a callback number that takes texts, the address, what is happening, whether neighbors are out too, whether they own the home, and when someone can let you in.

Should the person answering diagnose the problem?

No. They collect facts. Your electricians decide what it is and what is urgent.

Why ask if the neighbors have power?

If the whole street is out, it may be a utility outage. The caller should check with their power company before you send a truck.

Short answer

A reply is not a promotion, but rules still apply

A missed-call text answers a person who reached out to your shop first, about the call they just made. That is the kind of text the rules treat very differently from marketing texts sent to a list.

It still has to follow a few rules. Business texts sent through a system need carrier registration (A2P 10DLC). The text should name your shop, stay on the topic of their call, and let people reply STOP to opt out. The federal rules come from the Telephone Consumer Protection Act (TCPA) and the FCC. Some states add their own rules. If your texting goes beyond replies, talk to your attorney.

Checklist

Six things to check before you turn it on

  • Registration. The texting number is registered with the carriers for the use you actually have, like replying to missed calls. See 10DLC registration for electricians.
  • Your name in the text. The caller should know right away who is texting them.
  • On topic. The text is about their call. No coupons, no "while we have you" offers.
  • STOP and HELP. People can opt out with STOP and get help with HELP, and an opt-out is honored.
  • One reply, then wait. Send the text back, then let the caller answer. Don't start a series of texts they never asked for.
  • No sensitive details. Don't ask for card numbers, alarm codes, or other private info by text.

Questions owners ask

Is missed-call text back legal?

A text that replies to someone who just called you, about that call, is treated very differently from marketing. It still needs carrier registration (A2P 10DLC), your business name, and STOP to opt out. Some states add their own rules. This is general information, not legal advice.

Do I need permission to text back someone who called me?

The caller reached out to you first, and the text is about their call. Marketing texts need clearer permission than a reply. If you want to send promotions, ask your attorney first.

What is 10DLC, and does text back need it?

A2P 10DLC is how US carriers register businesses that send texts from a regular 10-digit number through a business system. Missed-call text back uses one, so it needs to be registered. Unregistered texts can be blocked.

Can I use the missed-call text list for promotions later?

That is marketing, which needs clearer permission than a reply. Keep text back for replies, and ask your attorney before sending promotions.

Voicemail greetings for electricians: daytime, after hours, and storm days

Many callers hang up instead of leaving a message. A good greeting is short, says who you are, and tells callers the fastest way to reach you. Here are greetings for the times you are most likely to miss calls.

What works

Keep every greeting to a few seconds

  • Say your shop name first, so callers know they reached the right place.
  • Say when they will hear back, and keep that promise.
  • Point them to text if your number takes texts. It is faster for both of you.
  • Ask for the address and what is going on, not a long story.
  • You can add a safety line, like telling callers to call 911 if there is fire or immediate danger.

Sample greetings

Four greetings you can adapt

Daytime, on a job

"You've reached [your shop name]. We're on a job. Text this number with what you need and your address, and we'll get back to you today."

Sets a clear promise.

After hours

"Thanks for calling [your shop name]. We're closed for the night. Leave your name, address, and what's going on, and we'll call you in the morning. If there is fire or immediate danger, call 911."

Honest about timing, with a safety line.

Holiday

"You've reached [your shop name]. We're closed for [holiday] and back on [day]. Leave a message or text this number, and we'll reply when we're back."

Change it back after the holiday.

Storm day

"This is [your shop name]. We're getting a lot of calls after the storm. Text us your address and what isn't working, and we'll reply in order. If neighbors are out too, check with your power company."

Helps you sort calls later.

Leave it out

What not to say

  • Long lists of services or your website address read slowly.
  • Promises you can't keep, like "We'll call back within the hour" on a busy day.
  • Anything that tells a caller a problem is safe.
  • Asking for card numbers or alarm codes.

Beyond voicemail

When voicemail isn't enough

A greeting only helps callers who stay on the line. Callers who hang up get nothing. A missed-call text back reaches every missed caller right away, whether or not they leave a message. See missed-call text examples for electricians and phone tips for one-truck electricians.

FAQ

Questions owners ask

What should an electrician's voicemail greeting say?

Your shop name, that you are on a job or closed, when they will hear back, and the fastest way to reach you, like texting the same number. Keep it to a few seconds.

Should my after-hours greeting mention emergencies?

You can add a safety line, like telling callers to call 911 if there is fire or immediate danger. Your shop decides how other urgent calls are handled.

Why do callers hang up instead of leaving a voicemail?

Many callers would rather try the next shop than wait. A short greeting helps the ones who stay. A missed-call text back reaches the ones who hang up.

How often should I change my greeting?

When your hours change, for holidays, and before big storms. Change it back after.

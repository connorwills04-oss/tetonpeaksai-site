Standby generator leads: how to follow up when buyers take weeks to decide

Generator calls spike after an outage, then the buyer slows down. They compare prices, talk it over at home, and ask about fuel and permits. Here is how to stay in touch through a long decision without being pushy.

The pattern

Why generator leads go quiet

Outage spikes

After a storm or a long outage, calls can pile up. Some callers are ready. Others are just starting to look.

Big decision

A standby generator is a large purchase. Buyers often need time and a second opinion.

More parties

Fuel supply, a transfer switch, permits, and sometimes the gas utility all have to line up.

First call

What to find out on the first call

  • What do you want to keep running? The whole house, or key circuits like the furnace, fridge, and well pump.
  • Fuel: Do they have natural gas, propane, or neither?
  • Timing: Do they want it before a season, or are they just pricing?
  • Where would it sit? Ask for a photo of the outside wall near the meter and gas line.
  • Decision makers: Is anyone else part of the decision?

Follow-up

A follow-up plan that fits a slow decision

  1. 01

    Same day

    Thank them and confirm what you'll send next, like a quote or a site visit time.

  2. 02

    After the quote

    A short check-in a few days later. Offer to answer questions about fuel, sizing, or permits.

  3. 03

    A couple of weeks out

    One more friendly note. Ask if the timing has changed.

  4. 04

    Before the next season

    If they went quiet, a seasonal reminder is fair. Then stop if they don't reply.

Sample texts

Follow-ups that don't nag

After the quote

"Hi [name], this is [your shop name]. Any questions on the generator quote? Happy to walk through sizing or fuel."

Short and useful. One question.

Two weeks later

"Hi [name], [your shop name] here. Just checking in on the generator. Still thinking it over, or should we close this out for now?"

Gives them an easy way to say not now.

Before the season

"Hi [name], it's [your shop name]. Storm season is coming up. Want us to take another look at your generator quote?"

Only send to people who agreed to hear from you.

Sample texts, not results from a client. Replace the words in [brackets], and approve the final wording.

Good to know

Keep it honest

  • Don't promise the generator will run everything unless you've sized it that way.
  • Permit and gas utility steps vary by area. Tell the buyer what applies in theirs.
  • Texts follow carrier rules. People can reply STOP to opt out. See business texting rules.

Example

A lead after an outage

After a two-day outage, a homeowner calls about a standby generator. You send a quote that week. They go quiet. A check-in a few days later gets a question about propane. You answer it. Two weeks later they say they want to wait until fall. In late summer, one reminder brings them back to schedule the install.

FAQ

Questions owners ask

How often should I follow up on a generator quote?

A few times, spaced out. For example, a few days after the quote, about two weeks later, and once before the next storm season. Stop when they reply or say no.

What should I ask on the first generator call?

Ask what they want to keep running, what fuel they have, their timing, where the unit would sit, and who else is part of the decision.

Can follow-up texts go out automatically?

Yes. With quote follow-up, texts and emails go out on a schedule you choose and stop as soon as the customer replies. You approve the wording.

Is it OK to text past generator leads before storm season?

Only text people who gave you their number and agreed to hear from you, and honor STOP. This is general information, not legal advice.

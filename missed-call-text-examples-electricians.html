The basics

What a good missed-call text does

  • Says who you are. Use your shop name, so the caller knows the text is from the business they called.
  • Says you got the call. The caller knows they reached you and don't need to try the next shop.
  • Asks one or two questions. What is going on, and the address. That is enough to plan a callback.
  • Keeps promises small. "Soon" or "in the morning" is safer than a set time you might miss.
  • Follows carrier rules. People can reply STOP to opt out and HELP for help. See business texting rules.

Sample texts

Five texts you can start from

Busy day

"Hi, this is [your shop name]. Sorry we missed your call. We're on a job right now. What can we help with? We'll call you back soon."

Use it during the workday, when you're on a service call and can't pick up.

After hours

"Hi, this is [your shop name]. We're closed for the night, but we got your call. Reply with what's going on and your address, and we'll get back to you in the morning. If there is fire or immediate danger, call 911."

Use it if many of your missed calls come at night or on weekends. The safety line is your call.

Storm day or outage

"Hi, this is [your shop name]. We're getting a lot of calls today and our crew is out. Reply with your address and what isn't working, and we'll call you back as soon as we can. For outage updates, check with your power company."

Use it when calls stack up after a storm. Asking for the address helps you sort calls later.

Estimate requests

"Hi, this is [your shop name]. Sorry we missed you. Is this about a quote, like a panel upgrade or an EV charger? Reply with a little about the job, and we'll set up a time."

Use it if many callers want quotes. Then see quote follow-up for the estimates you send.

Short and simple

"Thanks for calling [your shop name]. We'll call you back soon. If it's easier, reply here with what you need."

Use it if you want one text that works any time of day.

These are sample texts, not results from a client. You approve the final wording. Replace [your shop name] with your business name.

Leave it out

What not to put in the text

  • Don't diagnose the problem or tell callers something is safe. Your people make those calls.
  • Don't ask for card numbers or other private details by text.
  • Don't quote prices unless you are sure of them.
  • Don't make it long. A few short lines are easier to read on a phone.

FAQ

Questions owners ask

What should a missed-call text say for an electrician?

Keep it short. Say who you are and that you got the call. Ask what is going on and the address. Then say you will call back soon. You approve the wording before it goes out.

Should the text promise a callback time?

Only if you can keep it. "We'll call you back soon" is safer than a set time on a busy day.

What should an after-hours text say?

Say you got the call and when you will get back to them. You can add a safety line, like telling callers to call 911 if there is fire or immediate danger.

What should I leave out of a missed-call text?

Leave out anything that diagnoses the problem or says something is safe. Don't ask for card numbers or other private details by text. Keep prices out unless you are sure of them.

Are there rules for business texts?

Business texts have to be registered with the phone carriers (A2P 10DLC). Every text program should honor STOP to opt out and HELP for help. We handle the registration and build in STOP and HELP. This is general information, not legal advice.

Do I need new software or a new phone number?

No new software for you, and customers keep calling your same business number. Behind the scenes, we add a texting number for your business. Callers get the text from that number.
